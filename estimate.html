For every job: light and problem spots

Shoot on an overcast day or in the morning if you can — hard midday sun blows out siding and hides the chalking and cracking I'm looking for. Inside, turn all the lights on.

And at the end: close-ups of every problem spot — peeling paint, soft or rotted wood, cracked caulk, water stains, dents, holes, a patch someone else did. Get close enough to actually see it. These are the shots that keep my number honest, and they're the ones people leave out most often.

Better yet, shoot a video

A ninety-second video beats twenty photos.

Walk slow — slower than feels natural. Pan across whole walls instead of jumping around. Talk while you film: "this is the ceiling with the water stain," "this is the corner that keeps peeling." That narration is worth as much as the picture. Stop and hold three or four seconds on anything that looks like a problem. One clip per room, thirty to sixty seconds each.

Sending it: the upload above takes photos, not big video files. Text videos straight to me at (907) 414-7475, or email a link to abe@heritagepaintingandrenewal.com. If your phone says the file's too big to email, it'll offer to send a link instead — that works fine.

Or we can do it live. Call me and we'll set up a video call. You walk, I'll tell you where to point. Fifteen minutes, and it's the most accurate way to do this without me being there.

What happens next

I'll look everything over and get you a written price, usually in a day or two. It'll spell out what's included — the prep, the products, how many coats — not just a number at the bottom. If something in the photos doesn't add up, I'll call and ask rather than guess high.

Straight talk about photo estimates

A photo estimate is an estimate. I'll stand behind the number as long as what's under the paint matches what I could see.

Here's what can change it, and I'd rather say so now than argue about it later:

  • rot or soft wood that doesn't show in a picture
  • wallpaper under the paint
  • failed caulk you can't see from twenty feet
  • a deck that needs stripping and sanding, not just washing
  • an old finish that won't take a new coat without extra prep
  • lead-safe work on a pre-1978 house if the scope grows

If I run into any of that, I stop and show you before I do anything. You get a written change order with a price on it and you decide. No work happens on something you didn't agree to, and no surprise line items at the end.

On some jobs — usually older houses, or anything with a lot of unknowns — I'll give you a range instead of a single number and firm it up on day one. That's me being honest, not hedging.
