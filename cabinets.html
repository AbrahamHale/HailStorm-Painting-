Cabinets & finish work

Cabinets, trim, doors, and furniture

A kitchen repaint costs a fraction of new cabinets, but only if the finish is done right — cabinet doors see more handling than any other painted surface in a house, and a finish applied without proper preparation fails at the handles first.

Here's how I do it: everything gets degreased and scuff-sanded, then a bonding primer made for slick factory finishes, then the topcoats. Doors and drawer fronts come off and get sprayed flat — that's how you get the smooth factory look instead of brush marks. Boxes are masked and finished in place.

How I work

What a professional job includes

  • Preparation is most of the job.

    Degreasing, scuff-sanding every surface, and a bonding primer before any color — on cabinets, skipping any one of those is why finishes peel. Preparation decides whether the finish is still holding in ten years, and it's the first thing a low bid cuts.

  • Coats are determined by the job.

    Some jobs need two coats; some need four. It depends on the color change, the surface, and the product. I apply what the job requires — not a number picked to win a bid.

  • Your property is protected.

    I cover floors, mask what isn't being painted, move and protect furniture, and keep the site orderly for the duration of the work.

  • Final walkthrough.

    I walk the completed job and inspect it myself before I present it to you as finished.

  • Cleanup is part of the job.

    The site is left clean and ready to use.

If the job turns out bigger than the photos showed

If a job needs significantly more scraping or repair than what I could see when I quoted it, that's a separate charge — but I tell you before I do it, in writing, and you decide. Same rules as my photo estimates: no work happens on something you didn't agree to, and no surprise line items at the end.
