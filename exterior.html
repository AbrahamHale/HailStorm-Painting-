Exterior painting

Siding and trim that hold up to Minnesota

Sun on the south wall, ice on the north, and a freeze-thaw cycle that finds every crack in the caulk. Exterior paint here lives or dies on prep, so that's where I put the hours.

I paint wood, LP, cedar, and stucco — body, trim, soffit, fascia, doors, and garage doors. Whole houses, garages, cabins, and outbuildings.

How I work

Where the time actually goes

  • Prep is most of the job.

    Washing the siding first, scraping everything loose, sanding the edges smooth, caulking open joints, and priming bare wood before any finish coat goes on. It's where the time goes, and it decides whether the finish is still holding in ten years. It's also the first thing a cheap bid cuts.

  • Coats: what the job needs.

    Some jobs take two coats. Some take four. It depends on the color change, the surface, and the product. I put on what the job actually needs, not a number I picked to win a bid.

  • I take care of the place.

    Covering, masking, moving and protecting furniture, keeping the site clean while I work.

  • I check it before I call it done.

    I walk the job and go over it before I tell you it's finished.

  • I clean up.

    You get your place back.

If the job turns out bigger than the photos showed

If a job needs significantly more scraping or repair than what I could see when I quoted it, that's a separate charge — but I tell you before I do it, in writing, and you decide. Same rules as my photo estimates: no work happens on something you didn't agree to, and no surprise line items at the end.
