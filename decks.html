Decks, fences & log homes

Staining, sealing, and refinishing

Around here wood is half of what I work on — decks, fences, and log walls. Stain fails different from paint: it doesn't peel so much as wear away and let the water in. Catch it early and it's a wash and a coat. Wait too long and it's stripping, sanding, and starting over.

I'll tell you which one your deck actually is. A gray deck in a wide photo could be either — that's why I get down close on the boards before I price it.

Log homes

Log walls are their own trade: the right stain for the wood and exposure, chinking where it's failed, stripping when the old finish is done, and getting gables, dormers, and rafter tails while the equipment is up. If you've got a log place, send photos of the worst wall — usually the south one — and I'll tell you what it needs.

How I work

What a professional job includes

  • Preparation is most of the job.

    Washing off the gray and the loose finish, letting the wood dry down, sanding where the old coat won't hold, and masking everything the stain shouldn't touch. Preparation decides whether the finish is still holding in ten years, and it's the first thing a low bid cuts.

  • Coats are determined by the job.

    Some jobs need two coats; some need four. It depends on the color change, the surface, and the product. I apply what the job requires — not a number picked to win a bid.

  • Your property is protected.

    I cover floors, mask what isn't being painted, move and protect furniture, and keep the site orderly for the duration of the work.

  • Final walkthrough.

    I walk the completed job and inspect it myself before I present it to you as finished.

  • Cleanup is part of the job.

    The site is left clean and ready to use.

If the job turns out bigger than the photos showed

If a job needs significantly more scraping or repair than what I could see when I quoted it, that's a separate charge — but I tell you before I do it, in writing, and you decide. Same rules as my photo estimates: no work happens on something you didn't agree to, and no surprise line items at the end.
